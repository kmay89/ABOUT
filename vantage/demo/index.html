Where the Wave Pool Was

Twelve flights, one patch of sky, and eighteen months in which an abandoned waterpark was handed back to the lake.

April 2025 – September 2026 12 flights Simulated imagery

For eighteen summers the old waterpark on the south shore sat behind a chain-link fence, its wave pool going green at the deep end. Then the excavators came. Every six or seven weeks a drone returned to the same patch of sky to watch the shoreline come back: a public beach where the deep end was, a boardwalk where a dock had rotted, and a meadow where 135 cars used to park.

The site

Eighteen summers behind a fence

The waterpark opened in 1988 on the south shore, a wave pool fanned toward the water so that swimmers could see the lake from the deep end. It closed after the 20071 season and never reopened. By the spring of 2025 the slide tubes had faded to pastel, cottonwoods were rooting in the walkways, and the pool held a single green puddle where the wave machines used to be.

The plan that replaced it is modest on paper: keep the two gatehouses, remove almost everything else, and give the shoreline back. A beach where the pool was. A boardwalk in place of the dock. A meadow on the half of the parking lot nobody had needed in years.
