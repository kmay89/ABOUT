How the number works. Every game against the computer is rated, with or without a clock. Each coach has a rating of its own, so beating a stronger one moves you further than beating a gentler one, and your first few games move you most while the room is still finding your level. Hints and take-backs are always allowed — each one makes that game count for a little less (a take-back a fifth, a hint a tenth), because the number is meant to say what you can do on your own. Starting a new game is free while a game is young; once it is well under way, or you are clearly behind, leaving counts as a loss. Games between two people, lessons and opening stories never touch it.

Settings

The lookColours, material, board pattern, and which carved set the 3D board uses — change them while you play.
The coach's voiceHow much the room says, and in whose language. Everything it notices is kept in the Notes tab either way.
SoundEvery piece has its own voice: the material decides how it rings, the weight decides how low, and a piece that slides is heard sliding. All synthesized here — nothing to download.
Piece soundsLively gives every piece a game voice on top of its real one: a pawn's pip, a knight's clip-clop, a rook's drum, a bell for the king, a pop and a run of notes for every capture (higher for each in a row). Natural is just wood, ivory and glass.
HapticsFeel the board: a light tick as you pick a piece up, a knock as it lands, a real hit for a capture. Your own moves in full, the other side's softly.This device doesn't do haptics — everything else still works.
Tilt to look roundTip the phone and the 3D board answers, a degree or two, as if you had moved your head over a real one.Your phone asks first — tap to allow motion.
Clock skinSimple digits, or the classic tournament look.
Move dots & capture ringsLegal-move dots and capture rings when you pick up a piece.
Training wheelsShows what's in danger before you move: your pieces that can be won breathe, and a lifted piece shows where it would be lost. Never with a clock. A rated game played with them counts as 75%.
Drawing on the boardHold a square and drag to draw your own arrow — or right-drag with a mouse; let go where you started for a circle. Your drawing stays with that position. Turn it off if you like to rest a finger on a piece while you think.
Opponents talkThe coaches introduce themselves and say a word now and then: when they take something, when they lose something, at check and at the end. Never about what is on the board.
Momentum barA bar over the board that leans toward whoever is doing better, from what anybody can see: material, pieces in play, a king tucked away, and who just landed a blow. It never knows what the coach knows, so it is never a hint.
Coaches' boardsPlay each coach on its own board. Beat a coach once in a rated game and its board is yours, in The look, to use anywhere. Off keeps your own board for every game.
Spoken boardFor a screen reader: each move, the result, and anything you ask with a key are read out. Everything adds the coach's remarks. Nothing is ever spoken aloud otherwise.
PromotionAsk which piece every time, or make it a queen at once. Even then the room still asks when another piece would mate and the queen wouldn't, or when the queen would stalemate. Lessons always ask.

About this room

The Chess Room is part of The Solving Room — free things that teach, built plainly.

Everything runs in your browser: the rules engine (checked against millions of known-good positions), the 3D board (raw WebGL, no libraries — the pieces are carved by the browser at start-up, not downloaded), the coach, and the clock. Nearby play links two devices directly with WebRTC — the invite code is the connection; there is no server, no account, no tracking of any kind.

Install it (Add to Home Screen) and it works offline, and quietly tells you when a new version is ready.

MIT licensed. Be kind to your opponent — especially when you're winning.
