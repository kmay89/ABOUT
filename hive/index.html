Paused

  1. Tap a flower. The bees go and bring its nectar home. Tap it again to call them off.
  2. Tap the hive. The queen lays an egg. It costs honey, and it hatches into a bee.
  3. Tap a hornet. The bees nearby drop what they are doing and ball her.
  4. Be ready for winter. The snowflake at the top says how many bees and how much honey it will take. In winter, tap when the wind lands.

That is all of it. A year is six days: two of spring, two of summer, two of autumn. Each night the hive learns one new thing, and in spring it remembers two of them.

Bees decide the rest themselves. One finds a flower, the others follow once she has been there, and they share themselves out between everything you are paying attention to. More flowers than bees means everything goes slowly; choosing is the game.
