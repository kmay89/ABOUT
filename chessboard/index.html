    03 · How it works

    A latch you can feel.

    A tongue on the lid drops between two pairs of springs in the tray. A 27° ramp pushes the springs apart, a flat crest holds them, and then a 45° shoulder lets them close behind it, so the lid pulls itself shut. Drag the lid down, or press play, and watch the real section at true scale.

    Lid3.00 mm
    Spring deflection0.00 mm
    Push needed0 g

    Lid open: the tongue hasn't reached the springs.

    • ~330 g to close, at the latch
    • ~640 g to pull it open
    • 0.6 mm spring travel, 0.41 % strain
    • 0.08 mm preload when shut: no rattle
    • ~1 mJ released at the snap: an audible click
    • Hundreds of thousands of cycles in PLA at 12 MPa

    Grip from both sides

    The tongue is squeezed from left and right at once. Lid play from the hinge and pegs (±0.25 mm) only moves load from one side to the other; the total grip stays the same. One-sided snaps felt random from print to print.

    Long, soft springs

    0.6 mm of travel instead of 0.3, so a ±0.1 mm print error changes the force by ±17 % rather than ±33 %. It works on any reasonably tuned printer.

    Bend in the layer plane

    The beams flex sideways, so the stress runs along the extruded lines: the strongest, most fatigue-proof direction in an FDM part.

    A hinge that just worked

    Ten teardrop knuckles, printed in place, pinned with 22 cm of ordinary 1.75 mm filament. Swept 0–180° in simulation: zero collisions over 2.3 million sampled points.

    Design to the nozzle

    Springs exactly two lines wide, star points ≥ 0.6 mm, the ERRERLabs letters thickened to 0.5 mm hairlines and checked at one nozzle width. Every overhang 45° or less.

    One colour swap a layer

    The dark squares, coordinates, double rule and compass stars all live in the first four layers, so the AMS changes colour once a layer and then prints marble to the top.

    05 · Assembly

    Two minutes, no tools.

    1. Lay both halves tray-side up so the hinge knuckles interleave.
    2. Push a 1.75 mm filament pin in from the h-file end until it stops. Cut it flush and add a drop of thin CA.
    3. Lay the pieces in their nests: marble on the left, black on the right, bases toward the hinge.
    4. Close the lid until the latch clicks. To open it, pull up at the finger notch.

    Two colours on another slicer

    Load both STLs of a half as one object with multiple parts. Assign light → Marble (or White) and dark → Black. Every colour change happens in the first four layers, so there is one swap per layer and very little purge. Turn on Flush into object infill.

    Single-colour printer

    Print mono_half_A/B in a light colour and mono_inlay_tiles in a dark one. The board has 0.8 mm pockets; press each tile in with a drop of CA. The tiles print face-down too, so they match the board's surface.

    The pin won't go in

    Twist a 2 mm drill bit through the knuckles by hand.

    A peg won't seat

    You're over-extruding. Calibrate flow, or sand the peg lightly. (Swatch prints the coupons that find the right flow.)

    The latch is too stiff or too loose

    Set LATCH_DELTA in source/case.py: 0.5 is softer (~535 g hold), 0.6 the default (~640 g), 0.7 firmer (~750 g). Don't go above 0.75. Then rebuild with python3 make_3mf.py --no-hardware.

    The halves rock when open

    That's elephant's foot. Turn on its compensation (0.1–0.15 mm).

    Pieces too loose or tight in the nests

    C_NEST in source/case.py, 0.35 mm by default.

    Magnets and weighted pieces

    The generator can also build a Magnets + Weights edition: four 6 × 3 mm magnets in the corners and pieces with a hollow core for ~110 g of tungsten putty, with the print pauses set. Run python3 make_3mf.py (without --no-hardware) from the source download. The pauses: 7.80 mm and 15.60 mm on each half (magnets), and the first layer above 5.00 mm on the pieces (ballast).
